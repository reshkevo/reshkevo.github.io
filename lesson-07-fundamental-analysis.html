Fundamental analysis & the economic calendar

Charts show what price did. Fundamentals are a big part of why. Here are the releases that actually move currencies.

8 min readLesson 07 of 8
Advertisement

What moves a currency

A currency's value reflects demand for the economy behind it. Three forces dominate:

  • Interest rates: higher rates tend to attract capital seeking yield and support the currency.
  • Inflation: central banks raise rates to cool inflation and cut to support weak economies, so inflation data previews rate decisions.
  • Growth and employment: a strengthening economy tends to support its currency.

Central banks are the main character

The Federal Reserve, ECB, Bank of England, Bank of Japan and others set the rate policy that most other data feeds into. Their meetings and press conferences are among the highest-impact events on any calendar.

A distinction worth keeping

Markets often move more on whether a decision matched expectations than on the decision itself. A fully priced-in hike can produce a muted reaction while a surprise hold can move price sharply. The surprise matters, not the headline.

Reading an economic calendar

ReleaseWhat it signalsTypical impact
Interest rate decisionsCentral bank policy stanceHigh
CPI (inflation)Price pressure in the economyHigh
Non-farm payrolls / employmentLabour market healthHigh
GDPOverall economic growthMedium–High
PMIForward-looking business surveyMedium
Retail salesConsumer spending strengthMedium

Calendars show the forecast, the previous reading and the actual figure at release. The gap between forecast and actual usually drives the first few minutes. You can watch the live calendar on our markets page.

Trading around high-impact news

High-impact releases can bring sudden volatility, wider spreads and gaps through levels, conditions where a normal stop-loss can behave less predictably.

A risk-first way to handle news

Many risk-first traders avoid opening new positions in the minutes around a high-impact release and are deliberate about size on anything held through one. It is not about predicting the news. It is the Lesson 4 principle: stop and size must reflect the conditions the trade will actually face.

Beyond the calendar

Longer themes, such as a sustained shift in a central bank's path, geopolitics, or commodity trends for commodity-linked currencies like AUD or CAD, play out over weeks or months and form the trend context behind day-to-day price action.
