Trading journal template you can start today

A simple structure for logging every trade so your own data can teach you what works.

4 min readTools
Advertisement

Why journal at all

A journal turns a pile of outcomes into a dataset. Over time it answers questions no single trade can: which setups you execute well, what time of day you trade worst, and whether you cut winners short or let losers run.

The minimum to record for every trade

FieldExample
Date / sessionMon, London session
Pair and directionEUR/USD long
Setup and reasonPullback to 50 EMA in H4 uptrend
Entry / stop / target1.0850 / 1.0825 / 1.0925
Position size and % risked0.04 lots, 1%
Risk-reward1:3
Outcome+$30
Followed my plan? (Y/N)Y
Emotion and noteFelt hesitant at entry; waited for the candle to close

The weekly review

  1. How many trades did I take, and how many followed my plan?
  2. Which setup produced the best results? The worst?
  3. Did I break a risk rule? Why?
  4. What is the one thing I will do differently next week?
The most useful column

“Followed my plan?” A losing trade taken by the plan is fine. A winning trade taken by breaking the plan is a warning sign.

Copy this table into a spreadsheet or notebook. Consistency matters more than the tool.
