Technical indicators

Indicators do not predict the future. They summarize the past so it is easier to read at a glance.

8 min readLesson 06 of 8
Advertisement

What an indicator is

Every indicator is a formula run on price (and sometimes volume), replotted so a pattern becomes easier to see. Nothing on the chart is magic. Learn what an indicator calculates before you learn the signal it supposedly gives.

Moving averages: the trend, smoothed

A moving average plots the average price over the last N periods. The SMA weights every period equally. The EMA weights recent prices more and reacts faster. Price above a rising average is usually read as uptrend context. Crossovers are popular but lagging: by the time they happen, a chunk of the move has passed.

RSI: is this move stretched?

The Relative Strength Index measures the speed and size of recent moves on a 0 to 100 scale.

RSI readingCommon interpretation
Above 70Potentially overbought
Below 30Potentially oversold
Around 50No strong momentum
The trap to avoid

Overbought does not mean it must fall now. In a strong trend RSI can stay above 70 or below 30 for a long time while price keeps running. Treat extremes as a prompt to pay attention, not an automatic signal to fade the trend.

MACD: momentum shifting gears

MACD compares two EMAs (typically 12 and 26 periods) with a signal line and a histogram.

MACD line = EMA(12) − EMA(26)
Signal line = EMA(9) of the MACD line
Histogram = MACD line − Signal line

The MACD line crossing above its signal line suggests momentum turning up. A shrinking histogram while price keeps moving suggests fading momentum.

Bollinger Bands: how wide is normal right now?

A moving average with two bands set by recent volatility. They widen when volatility rises and narrow when it falls. Price touching a band is not automatically a signal because in strong trends price can walk the band. A sharp narrowing (the squeeze) often comes before a bigger move, direction unknown.

Fewer indicators, used well

Six overlapping indicators usually produce conflict, not clarity. A workable setup is one tool for trend context, one for momentum, and price action itself (support, resistance, candle structure) as the tie-breaker.

Where this connects

Indicators describe conditions. They never replace the trend-and-levels read from Lesson 3 or the position sizing from Lesson 4.
