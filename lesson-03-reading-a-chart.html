Reading a chart

Before any strategy you need the vocabulary to describe what price is doing. This is that vocabulary.

8 min readLesson 03 of 8
Advertisement

Anatomy of a candlestick

A candlestick summarizes price over one period. Each candle shows four prices:

  • Open: where the period started
  • High: the highest price reached
  • Low: the lowest price reached
  • Close: where the period ended

The thick part is the body (open to close). The thin lines are wicks. A close above the open is usually drawn in one colour, a close below in another.

Reading the shape

A long body shows decisive movement. Long wicks with a small body show price pushed one way and then rejected, a tug of war that ended near where it started.

Timeframes change the story, not the facts

TimeframeEach candleCommon use
M1 – M151–15 minutesVery short-term entries; needs full attention
H1 – H41–4 hoursSwing entries within a day or two
D11 dayDaily trend and key levels
W11 weekLong-term structure and context

A practical habit: check a higher timeframe such as D1 for the trend and key levels, then drop to H1 to time an entry. Trading a 5-minute chart with no idea what the daily looks like is trading half blind.

Support and resistance

Support is a level where buying has previously stopped a decline. Resistance is where selling has stopped a rally. Think of them as zones, not exact lines. Once resistance breaks decisively it often becomes support on a pullback, and vice versa. Our retest strategy guide builds on this.

Trend structure

  • Uptrend: higher highs and higher lows.
  • Downtrend: lower highs and lower lows.
  • Range: price oscillating between support and resistance with no directional structure.
A grounding habit

Before any entry ask three questions: what is the higher-timeframe trend, where are the nearest support and resistance, and does my trade agree with or fight both answers? That alone filters out a large share of low-quality trades.
