Practise first: your $10,000 virtual-funds demo account

A demo account lets you rehearse your plan with virtual money before a single real dollar is at risk.

6 min readGetting started
Advertisement

Why a demo comes before a live account

Everything in the course works on paper. A demo account lets you test whether you can actually follow it: place trades, set stops, size positions and sit through losing streaks with no real money at stake.

Our partner broker Exness offers a demo with $10,000 in virtual funds, so you can learn the platform with a market leader.

What to practise

  1. Sizing every trade with the risk calculator, risking the same 1% you would on a live account.
  2. Placing the stop first, before you think about the target.
  3. Journaling every trade with the journal template.
  4. Trading one setup from your written plan until you can execute it without hesitating.

When is it time to go live?

CheckReady when
Sample sizeAt least 30–50 demo trades logged
Rule followingYou followed your plan on the large majority of them
RiskYou never exceeded your per-trade and weekly risk limits
EmotionsYou are comfortable with a losing streak on paper
Demo is not live

Demo fills are cleaner and the money is not yours, so emotions are muted. When you go live, start with the smallest size your broker allows. The point is to rehearse your discipline under real stakes, not to make money on day one.

Choosing a broker: what to look for

  • Regulation in a reputable jurisdiction and clear client-fund policies
  • Transparent spreads, commissions and swap fees
  • Deposit and withdrawal methods that work where you live (including local mobile money or bank options)
  • A platform you find clear and a demo that mirrors live conditions

Whichever broker you choose, check the terms yourself. Trading leveraged products carries a high level of risk and is not suitable for everyone.
