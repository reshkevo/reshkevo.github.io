What is forex, actually?

Before charts and strategy, you need to know what market you are standing in. Here is the mechanical picture.

6 min readLesson 01 of 8
Advertisement

The market with no building

The foreign exchange market, forex or FX, is where one currency is exchanged for another. There is no central exchange floor. It is a decentralized, over-the-counter network of banks, brokers and trading platforms connected electronically and running about 24 hours a day on weekdays.

By trading volume it is the largest financial market in the world, with daily turnover measured in the trillions of dollars. On the major pairs there is almost always enough liquidity to enter and exit without your own order moving the price.

Who is actually in the market

  • Central banks manage reserves and sometimes intervene to influence their own currency.
  • Commercial banks are the backbone, trading for clients and for themselves.
  • Corporations hedge currency risk from international trade.
  • Funds trade currencies as an asset class, often in large size over long horizons.
  • Retail traders are individuals trading through a broker, usually on shorter timeframes and smaller size. That is most of us.

Retail traders are a small fraction of volume. The market is not designed around us, so managing risk sensibly is entirely our responsibility.

Currencies always come in pairs

You cannot buy dollars in isolation. Every quote is a pair: a base currency and a quote currency.

EUR/USD = 1.0850
base = EUR · quote = USD · 1 euro costs 1.0850 US dollars

If you buy EUR/USD you buy euros and sell dollars, and profit if the euro strengthens. If you sell it you do the reverse.

Majors, minors and exotics

CategoryExamplesTypical trait
MajorsEUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD, USD/CHFInclude USD, highest liquidity, tightest spreads
Minors / crossesEUR/GBP, AUD/JPY, GBP/JPYNo USD leg, still liquid, slightly wider spreads
ExoticsUSD/TRY, USD/ZAR, USD/KESA major against an emerging-market currency; wider spreads, sharper moves

The market runs in sessions

Because it spans time zones, trading is described in four overlapping sessions: Sydney (quiet, opens the week), Tokyo (active for JPY and Asia-Pacific pairs), London (the busiest session) and New York (overlaps London for a few hours). See our sessions guide in East Africa Time for exact local hours.

Why this matters

The London and New York overlap is usually when spreads are tightest and moves are cleanest on the majors. Same chart, different personality at 3am.
