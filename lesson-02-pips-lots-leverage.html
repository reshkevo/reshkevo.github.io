Pips, lots & leverage

The units that turn a price move on a screen into an actual dollar amount in your account.

7 min readLesson 02 of 8
Advertisement

What a pip is

A pip is the standard unit for measuring a price move. For most pairs it is the fourth decimal place. For pairs involving the yen it is the second decimal place.

EUR/USD 1.0850 → 1.0860 = 10 pips
USD/JPY 148.20 → 148.50 = 30 pips

Many brokers show an extra digit, a pipette, which is one tenth of a pip.

What a lot is

A lot is a standardized trade size.

Lot typeUnits of base currencyApprox. value per pip (EUR/USD)
Standard lot100,000$10
Mini lot10,000$1
Micro lot1,000$0.10

Lot size, not the number of trades, is the real lever on your risk. A 20-pip stop on 1 standard lot risks about $200. The same stop on 1 micro lot risks about $2.

Where this connects

This is the math behind our risk calculator: it takes your account, risk percentage and stop distance and works backward to the lot size.

Leverage: borrowed size, not free money

Leverage lets you control a large position with a small amount of your own capital, expressed as a ratio like 1:50 or 1:500. Your broker sets aside a portion of your account, the margin, to open the trade.

Margin required = Position size ÷ Leverage
$100,000 position at 1:100 = $1,000 margin

Leverage does not change how far a currency moves. It changes how large your profit or loss is relative to your account, in both directions equally.

A worked example

You open 1 standard lot of EUR/USD ($10 per pip) on a $2,000 account:

  • 20 pips in your favour: +$200, a 10% gain.
  • 20 pips against you: -$200, a 10% loss.
  • 100 pips against you with no stop: -$1,000, half the account gone.
Why this lesson gets skipped

Leverage is often marketed purely as opportunity. Mechanically it is neutral: it magnifies whatever happens. High leverage with no stop and an oversized lot is the most common way retail accounts die. High leverage with a small, deliberately sized position is just efficient use of capital. The risk is the position size you choose.

Margin calls, briefly

If losses erode your equity toward the margin in use, the broker may issue a margin call and eventually close trades automatically (a stop-out). It is a safety net, not a strategy. Good risk management means never getting near it.
