Trend pullback strategy: trade with the trend, not against it

A simple rules-based approach: identify the trend, wait for a pullback, enter with a defined stop.

7 min readStrategies
Advertisement

The idea

Instead of guessing tops and bottoms, trade in the direction of an established trend and enter on a temporary pullback. You get a closer stop and a better risk-reward than chasing the move.

The rules

  1. Trend filter (H4 or D1): price above a rising 50 EMA for longs, below a falling 50 EMA for shorts, with clear higher highs and lows (or lower highs and lows).
  2. Pullback (H1): price retraces toward the 50 EMA or a prior support/resistance zone.
  3. Trigger: a candle that closes back in the trend direction (a rejection wick or engulfing candle).
  4. Stop: just beyond the pullback's swing low (longs) or high (shorts).
  5. Target: at least 2 times the risk, or the next key level.
  6. Size: calculated from the stop with the risk calculator, risking 1% or less.

Example

EUR/USD is in an H4 uptrend. Price pulls back to the 50 EMA near 1.0850 and prints a bullish rejection candle. The stop goes at 1.0825 (25 pips). With a $1,000 account and 1% risk, the size is 0.04 lots. The target is 1.0925, giving 1:3.

When it fails

In a sideways market the trend filter keeps you out, which is the point. If price slices through the average with no pullback, you simply have no trade. Sitting out is a position.
